# Polyhedron Flake

Choose a solid. Replace it with one smaller copy at each corner. Each copy keeps the original orientation, and the next step repeats the replacement inside it.<sup><a id="polyhedron-flake-ref-1" href="#polyhedron-flake-source-1">1</a></sup>

<figure data-construction-player="polyhedron-flake"></figure>

An **octahedron** has eight triangular faces and six corners. This version makes six half-size copies. The gaps between them are small tetrahedra, which are removed at every step.

Try a **dodecahedron**, which has twenty corners, then an **icosahedron**, which has twelve. The dodecahedron makes more copies, but they are smaller. Together, they keep less of the original volume.

More pieces can still mean less material. Repeat either replacement and the total volume approaches zero: the pieces' volumes shrink faster than their number grows.

The [Sierpinski tetrahedron](/sierpinski-tetrahedron) uses the same corner-copy construction with four half-size tetrahedra.
