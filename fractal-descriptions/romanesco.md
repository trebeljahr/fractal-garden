# Romanesco

Look at one of the pointed bumps on a Romanesco cauliflower. Smaller bumps cover its sides. Those bumps carry smaller ones again. Cones and spiral rows appear at several sizes.<sup><a id="romanesco-ref-1" href="#romanesco-source-1">1</a></sup>

Start this model with one cone. Place 55 smaller cones around its sides, making them smaller as you move toward the tip. Repeat the arrangement on each new cone. The original cones stay in place.

<figure data-construction-player="romanesco"></figure>

Each new cone turns about 137.5 degrees around its parent. This is called the golden angle. Similar angles occur between successive leaves or flowers in many plants.<sup><a id="romanesco-ref-2" href="#romanesco-source-2">2</a></sup> View the model from above to see how the cones line up in curved rows.

The construction player shows two rounds of smaller cones. The 3D explorer adds finer copies as you approach. This is a geometric model inspired by Romanesco, not a simulation of how the plant grows. A real cauliflower has a smallest bump.
