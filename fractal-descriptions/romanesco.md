# Romanesco

Follow a curved row of bumps around a Romanesco cauliflower. Other rows turn the opposite way. Each bump carries smaller spiral rows of its own.<sup><a id="romanesco-ref-1" href="#romanesco-source-1">1</a></sup>

Start with a narrow cone. Place smaller cones around it. Their tips trace a wider cone, while their bases overlap the narrow one. Turn each new cone about 137.5 degrees and make it smaller toward the tip. This turn is called the golden angle.<sup><a id="romanesco-ref-2" href="#romanesco-source-2">2</a></sup> Repeat the arrangement around each smaller cone.

<figure data-construction-player="romanesco"></figure>

Viewed from above, this model has eight spiral rows in one direction and thirteen in the other. Eight and thirteen are neighbouring Fibonacci numbers. The same two sets of spirals repeat on each smaller head.<sup><a id="romanesco-ref-3" href="#romanesco-source-3">3</a></sup>

This is a geometric model inspired by Romanesco. A real cauliflower has a smallest bump.
