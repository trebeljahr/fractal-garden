# Koch Surface

Split a triangle into four smaller triangles. Keep the three at the corners. On the middle one, raise a triangular pyramid. Its three sloping sides replace the flat middle.<sup><a id="koch-surface-ref-1" href="#koch-surface-source-1">1</a></sup>

One flat triangle has become six. Repeat on every triangle.

<figure data-construction-player="koch-surface"></figure>

The new triangles have half the edge length and one quarter of the area. Six quarter-size areas add up to one and a half old areas. Each round makes the surface 50% larger.

The bumps keep shrinking, so the shape stays bounded even while its total triangle area grows without end.<sup><a id="koch-surface-ref-2" href="#koch-surface-source-2">2</a></sup>

## Four rough faces can fill out a cube

Choose the tetrahedron start to put the rule on all four faces of a triangular pyramid.

As the bumps accumulate, the solid approaches a cube. The original pyramid's four corners become alternating corners of that cube.<sup><a href="#koch-surface-source-2">2</a></sup>

The little triangles also leave structure inside the cube. Adding up every triangle's area therefore does not measure only the cube's outside.

The [quadratic Koch surface](/quadratic-koch-3d) raises cube-shaped bumps from squares. The [Mosely snowflake](/mosely-snowflake) makes a Koch shape another way, through its shadow.
