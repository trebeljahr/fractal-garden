# Fern 3

Each piece of this plant carries two long side branches and two shorter ones. The same unequal arrangement appears again inside those branches.

## Unequal branches from one rule

The rule `F → F[+FF][-FF]F[-F][+F]F` draws nine forward strokes. Three form the main stem, four form the long branch pair, and two form the short pair.

<figure data-investigation="fern-3"></figure>

All nine strokes expand at the next rewrite. At a fixed stem height, each child stroke is a third of the old stroke length.

The resulting plant has repeated structure without separate symbols for stems and tips. [Fern 2](/l-system/fern-2) uses two growing symbols instead. Both follow the bracketed constructions in [The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/abop/abop-ch1.pdf).
