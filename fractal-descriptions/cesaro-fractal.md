# Cesàro Fractal

Thin inward spikes almost meet inside this square. Their depth comes from changing one bend angle in a four-segment replacement.

## A sharper bend crowds the copies

For bend $\alpha$, four equal strokes span $2(1+\cos\alpha)$ stroke lengths. Holding that span fixed gives a child scale of $r=1/[2(1+\cos\alpha)]$.

<figure data-investigation="cesaro-fractal"></figure>

The corresponding similarity dimension is $D=\log4/\log[2(1+\cos\alpha)]$. At 60 degrees it equals the Koch value. As the bend approaches 90 degrees, the value approaches 2.

The [Koch anti-snowflake](/l-system/koch-anti-snowflake) keeps a fixed 60-degree bend. Cesàro’s adjustable bend shows how the copy size and the gaps change together.
