# GPU recursive surfaces

`GpuSpatialExplorer` renders Jerusalem, the five polyhedron flakes, both triangular Koch seeds the quadratic Koch surface and Romanesco. A full-screen WebGL 2 shader traverses their recursive constructions for each visible pixel. Camera movement never requests a triangle mesh or waits for a geometry worker. Romanesco’s finite construction player uses the same shader with a fixed rank and growth of the newest height term. Other construction players keep their existing generators.

`model.ts` describes each shape with a convex descendant hull, a seed surface and a short list of child transforms. Jerusalem keeps eight corner cubes at `sqrt(2)-1` and twelve edge cubes at its square; their ranks advance by one and two. Flakes use the existing seed vertices and ratios. Triangular Koch patches replace one triangle with six half-size triangles. Quadratic patches replace a square with eight flat squares, one raised top and four side walls, all at one third of its size.

Romanesco uses a continuous radial height field in `romanesco-surface.frag`, with the CPU reference and constants in `romanescoSurface.ts`. Golden-angle logarithmic coordinates fold into nearest hexagonal cells, giving the eight/thirteen spiral families. Each cell has a conical height plus a smaller copy of the same spiral field. The nested term vanishes at cell edges; the primary heights agree there. Each level's slope is about 0.84 of its parent's (`patchEpsilon` .14). A smaller ratio flattens the surface quickly: at .36, florets faded to a blank wall by zoom 16, and turning there looked like skewing a flat picture. Up to 64 nesting levels cover the supported camera range; pixel-sized bounds stop evaluation of unresolved terms. A bounded shear tilts the heads upward. A circular underside meets the side on the same scalloped ring. No cone instances or triangle meshes are constructed.

Hill climbing measures a largest raw side gradient of 2.90 and underside gradient of 2.47; the side is divided by 3.25 and the underside by 2.75. These margins are measured, not proved: the worst-case products are about 7 and 25 and would slow every frame. Their maximum is a signed lower-distance field. An analytic cone/cylinder interval clips empty space before conservative marching. A certified monotone front band can use bisection. Over-relaxed steps require overlapping empty balls. Difficult grazing rays continue in separate GPU passes with budgets 96, 256, 1024 and 4096. Restart distances retain all Float32 bits in two RGBA8 textures. Finished pixels discard before the next march; every pass finishes before presentation. Moving and resting views use the same framebuffer and detail cutoff.

Deep Romanesco frames use fixed-point CPU anchors before conversion to Float32. Smooth regions, apex continuations and tip deficits have separate coordinate programs. The general nonlinear program transports small coordinate differences through side, ring and underside reference orbits. Its 20×193 RGBA32F table contains conditioned scales and edge gaps, not a sampled surface. This avoids subtracting large absolute coordinates, including near cell edges. Every sampled field carries a numerical error allowance; marching subtracts it from the distance bound. The high-precision GPU oracle checks that allowance separately from hit accuracy. A 129-unit chart includes the 128-unit horizon and the finite-difference normal halo.

The triangular Koch descendant hull is the tetrahedron above its canonical triangle, with apex height `sqrt(2/3)/2` for a unit edge. The quadratic hull is `[-1,1] × [-1,1] × [0,1]` for a square of half-width one. Child hull containment is verified independently in the tests. A ray tests the actual triangle, square or convex seed when it reaches the requested detail, rather than intersecting an enclosing hull as if it were the surface.

The CPU retains the existing BigInt camera and selects a small set of local roots. It uses the original construction operations before subtracting the eye: fixed seed anchors for flakes, unequal ranks for Jerusalem, exact division by three for quadratic patches and actual triangle endpoints for triangular Koch. This preserves nearby boundaries through 240 zoom doublings. A frontier with twice the render horizon can survive small moves and turns; its fixed-point roots are rebased without cumulative float transforms. Visible subdivision stays on the GPU.

The quadratic surface compiles a specialized shader with the same traversal and detail rules. Its box hull uses three vector slab intervals, preserving signed division, parallel-ray tolerance and entry-normal tie order. Child centers identify the thirteen signed axis transforms, reducing each child from four texture fetches to one. The existing stack cursor also stores the ray octant. Child hulls are tested before writing ray stacks, and failed children still consume their original logical visit budget. Only the nearest hit replays its path to reconstruct normals, including when a traversal budget expires. Other shapes retain their original hull and child-transform paths. Flakes compile with a fixed construction kind; Jerusalem retains generic arithmetic because fixed-kind compilation changed Float32 output in image comparisons.

The triangular Koch surface also compiles a specialized shader. Its hulls overlap, so visit order decides the cost. Each node tests its six child hulls in parent coordinates, using uniform planes, and visits the survivors nearest first. A typical ray then makes one dive of about twelve nodes with a single hit. Child frames are uniforms, the stack holds 24 levels, and only the nearest hit replays its path for normals. `check-spatial-flight-gpu.mjs` tests this shader against the finite surfaces and at zooms 80 and 240.

The shader follows every intersected branch that could beat the nearest hit. Octant orders visit nearby children first, including when a child rotates the ray. Detail stops before a child's smallest feature becomes smaller than two display pixels, adjusted for the actual framebuffer resolution. This avoids sampling tiny holes as speckles. Rendering follows native display density within a fixed 32,000,000-pixel allocation and GPU size limits. The same policy applies during movement and at rest; frame timing never reduces quality. PNG export requests at least 4096 pixels on the longer edge, within the allocation and GPU limits, then restores the live framebuffer.

The drawable canvas fills the viewport. The shared framing projection fits the opening shape between controls without clipping the drawing area; rendering, picking and contact shading use the same projection. Movement, resting and PNG export use the same framebuffer resolution. Every changed camera view is rendered; an unchanged view stays idle.

The other recursive surfaces use a bounded visit count and up to forty local stack levels. Their existing per-ray budget fallback and CPU root-selection limits remain unchanged. Romanesco does not use the cone-child stack, BVH or seed-path prepass.

One GPU pass writes colour plus packed view-space normal/depth. The second pass applies the existing screen-space Soft or Strong corner shading. Koch facet lighting fades from its enclosing patch normal over one to three detail footprints. On the triangular surface a slanted patch counts as narrower, by the square root of its facing, which stops grazing rows of bumps from aliasing into stripes. Contact shading fades below eight projected leaf pixels and disappears below two. The triangular surface skips that fade and shades contact from the resolved normal: its leaf size halves where the detail level changes, so a fade by leaf size draws a visible band along each change. These fades change shading only. Classic and Depth lighting, outlines, independently eased atmosphere, PNG export and shared camera links remain available.

Picking uses a small GPU query target. Romanesco collision checks evaluate the same finite height field on the CPU, without pixel-tail pruning or a GPU readback. A fixed-point anchor retains deep coordinates; its signed lower-distance value supplies the safe movement radius. Other shapes retain their GPU clearance queries. Movement consumes the full elapsed interval in bounded steps and rechecks clearance between steps.

Romanesco reuses a certified clearance ball between CPU queries. A distance lower bound at one eye remains valid after subtracting the distance travelled. Fixed-point subtraction precedes conversion to floating point, and the result scales with camera zoom. Reuse stops after consuming 20% of the original ball. Geometry changes invalidate it; zero or failed queries are never reused. Visible surface evaluation remains entirely on the GPU.

`query.ts` is a small CPU ray reference for validation. Production rendering, picking and clearance do not use it. The independent finite polygon oracle comes from the existing construction generators. The old Romanesco cone catalogue and its finite union remain legacy reference utilities; they do not define the production surface. `romanesco-surface.test.mjs` and the two surface shader checks validate the new model.

Validation:

- `node --test tests/romanesco-surface.test.mjs tests/spatial-gpu.test.mjs tests/spatial-flight-timing.test.mjs tests/spatial-zoom.test.mjs`
- `node scripts/check-romanesco-surface.mjs CALLBACK.js BASE_URL` generates actual GPU field, first-hit, growth, seam and full-resolution motion checks. Run with Playwright CLI `run-code --filename` and require `passed: true`.
- `node scripts/check-romanesco-residual.mjs CALLBACK.js` generates fixed-point oracle comparisons for reference-coordinate fields at zooms 9–240. It reports unreliable evaluations independently of numerical errors.
- `node scripts/check-spatial-flight-gpu.mjs /private/tmp/check-spatial-flight-gpu.js` produces a Playwright callback comparing actual GPU hits with independent finite surfaces, deep camera frames and analytical edge tangencies. Run it with Playwright CLI `run-code --filename`; require `passed: true`. Hit/miss agreement is exact; depth tolerance is 0.0002 plus 8 ppm for Float32 division at grazing incidence.
- Add `--specialized` to check the production flake programs. `--diagnostics` reports matched BVH/linear traversal counts without adding counters to production shaders.
- `node scripts/check-romanesco-performance.mjs CALLBACK.js BASE_URL CAPTURE_DIRECTORY` measures synchronized full redraws at native Retina density. Timing is a measurement, not a fixed gate. The earlier exact cone-traversal comparison is a legacy experiment and is not a validation of the new surface.
- `node scripts/check-spatial-flight-ui.mjs /private/tmp/check-spatial-flight-ui.js BASE_URL CAPTURE_DIRECTORY` produces a bounded browser callback covering all nine variants, flight, saved views, context recovery, phone touch movement and PNG export. It records synchronized draw times as measurements, without a fixed performance gate.
- Run the project typecheck and production build after renderer or integration changes.

Exact-output performance comparisons:

- Save `utils/shaders/spatial-flight.frag` from the revision being compared into a baseline file.
- `QK_WIDTH=1280 QK_HEIGHT=720 QK_SAMPLES=16 QK_WARMUP=8 node scripts/check-quadratic-koch-performance.mjs BASELINE.frag /private/tmp/quadratic-performance.js` generates a Playwright callback. Run with `run-code --filename` and require `passed: true`.
- The callback alternates baseline/current draws in one WebGL context. Hardware timer queries measure the trace and production screen pass; one-pixel readback also records synchronized wall time. Compilation, captures and correctness probes are outside the measurements. `gl.finish()` alone does not synchronize Chromium reliably.
- Seven fixtures cover opening, rotation, close-up, both lighting styles, outlines and zoom depths 80/240. Every color and packed normal/depth byte must match. Picking, clearance and fallback diagnostics are compared separately. `QK_FILTER`, `QK_WIDTH`, `QK_HEIGHT`, `QK_SAMPLES` and `QK_WARMUP` bound reruns; `--candidate FILE` tests a standalone shader experiment.
- An optional third positional capture directory saves one `captures.json` download. Run `node scripts/check-quadratic-koch-performance.mjs --extract-captures CAPTURE_DIRECTORY/captures.json` to recover the original before/after PNGs. Capture failures are reported separately from completed correctness and timing results.

- Set `SPATIAL_SPECS=all` to compare all ten recursive constructions, or use comma-separated names from the harness. This mode independently reproduces the comparison baseline's quadratic specialization and Romanesco stack selection. Run bounded groups when collecting captures. `QK_GROWTH`, `QK_MAX_RANK` and `QK_VISIT_LIMIT` exercise partial construction and exhausted traversal paths; visit overrides affect both implementations and are test-only.
- `scripts/check-voxel-performance.mjs` provides paired shader measurements for all four voxel patterns, with `VOXEL_RAYS=9` covering full sample accumulation. Its style-specialization flag is experimental; no voxel shader changes were retained by this optimization.
- Finite construction faces use stable radix ordering over their existing Float32 depths. Equal depths preserve draw order, signed zeros remain ties, and NaNs keep the original comparator fallback. `node scripts/benchmark-face-depth-order.mjs` measures sorting; `node scripts/check-face-render-performance.mjs CALLBACK.js [CAPTURES]` compares complete Canvas2D draws against an independently restored native sort, including exact image bytes.

- 3D polylines retain growable projection and counting-sort buffers per canvas through a WeakMap. Clipping flags and active bin counts reset each draw; Float32 projection and stable stroke order remain unchanged. `node scripts/check-polyline-performance.mjs BASELINE_POLYLINE.ts CALLBACK.js [CAPTURES]` compares complete Lorenz, Rössler and Hilbert draws, including every moving frame and separately instrumented typed-array allocations. `tests/polyline-buffer.test.mjs` covers clipping, point-count changes, independent canvases and both depth-bin modes. Normalization reads indexed source points without allocating a typed-array view for each point.
