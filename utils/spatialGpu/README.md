# GPU recursive surfaces

`GpuSpatialExplorer` renders Jerusalem, the five polyhedron flakes, both triangular Koch seeds the quadratic Koch surface and Romanesco. A full-screen WebGL 2 shader traverses their recursive constructions for each visible pixel. Camera movement never requests a triangle mesh or waits for a geometry worker. Romanesco’s finite construction player uses the same shader with a fixed rank and base-anchored growth. Other construction players keep their existing generators.

`model.ts` describes each shape with a convex descendant hull, a seed surface and a short list of child transforms. Jerusalem keeps eight corner cubes at `sqrt(2)-1` and twelve edge cubes at its square; their ranks advance by one and two. Flakes use the existing seed vertices and ratios. Triangular Koch patches replace one triangle with six half-size triangles. Quadratic patches replace a square with eight flat squares, one raised top and four side walls, all at one third of its size.

Romanesco retains an analytic circular cone at each rank. Golden-angle transforms place 89 tilted florets along opposing eight/thirteen spiral rows. A rank-zero similarity about the apex continues the same sequence indefinitely, so there is no final smooth tip. This continuation adds no core or underside of its own. Every ordinary floret also has 22 downward buds whose overlapping sections cover its base disk. The same arrangement repeats on every bud. All transforms are uniform similarities; the florets are 15% wider than the preceding model.

A clipped conical envelope contains every descendant, including the underside buds. Its cylindrical cap and threaded child BVH skip disjoint groups before exact cone intersections. Deep local frames retain large ancestor cores as rebased quadratic coefficients, avoiding cancellation from their distant centres. An eye inside the solid uses connected ray intervals to find the union's outer exit, so overlapping florets do not expose internal seams.

Romanesco stores its fixed transforms and outward-rounded child bounds in three uniform buffers, each within WebGL 2's guaranteed 16 KiB block size. A GPU prepass shares visibility work across eight-by-eight pixel tiles. It keeps at most fifteen complete subtrees per tile; overflow keeps a larger subtree or uses the ordinary traversal. A retained convex core intersecting all four corner rays certifies an upper depth bound for every pixel in the tile. Fine pixels still evaluate their own exact surface and detail cutoff. Prefixes carry retained ancestor cores, including when a continuation has no visible children. An exact depth tie reruns that pixel in the original traversal order. Normal reconstruction runs once for the winning surface.

The triangular Koch descendant hull is the tetrahedron above its canonical triangle, with apex height `sqrt(2/3)/2` for a unit edge. The quadratic hull is `[-1,1] × [-1,1] × [0,1]` for a square of half-width one. Child hull containment is verified independently in the tests. A ray tests the actual triangle, square or convex seed when it reaches the requested detail, rather than intersecting an enclosing hull as if it were the surface.

The CPU retains the existing BigInt camera and selects a small set of local roots. It uses the original construction operations before subtracting the eye: fixed seed anchors for flakes, unequal ranks for Jerusalem, exact division by three for quadratic patches and actual triangle endpoints for triangular Koch. This preserves nearby boundaries through 240 zoom doublings. A frontier with twice the render horizon can survive small moves and turns; its fixed-point roots are rebased without cumulative float transforms. Visible subdivision stays on the GPU.

The quadratic surface compiles a specialized shader with the same traversal and detail rules. Its box hull uses three vector slab intervals, preserving signed division, parallel-ray tolerance and entry-normal tie order. Child centers identify the thirteen signed axis transforms, reducing each child from four texture fetches to one. The existing stack cursor also stores the ray octant. Child hulls are tested before writing ray stacks, and failed children still consume their original logical visit budget. Only the nearest hit replays its path to reconstruct normals, including when a traversal budget expires. Other shapes retain their original hull and child-transform paths. Flakes compile with a fixed construction kind; Jerusalem and triangular Koch retain generic arithmetic because fixed-kind compilation changed Float32 output in image comparisons.

The shader follows every intersected branch that could beat the nearest hit. Octant orders visit nearby children first, including when a child rotates the ray. Detail stops before a child's smallest feature becomes smaller than two display pixels, adjusted for the actual framebuffer resolution. This avoids sampling tiny holes as speckles. Rendering follows native display density within a fixed 32,000,000-pixel allocation and GPU size limits. The same policy applies during movement and at rest; frame timing never reduces quality. PNG export requests at least 4096 pixels on the longer edge, within the allocation and GPU limits, then restores the live framebuffer.

The drawable canvas fills the viewport. The shared framing projection fits the opening shape between controls without clipping the drawing area; rendering, picking and contact shading use the same projection. Movement, resting and PNG export use the same framebuffer resolution. Every changed camera view is rendered; an unchanged view stays idle.

Traversal has a bounded visit count and up to forty local stack levels. Romanesco compiles its own shader and selects 8, 12, 16, 24 or 40 stack slots from the actual root sizes, maximum child scale and minimum detail footprint. Distance to the enclosing hull can prove a larger minimum footprint for exterior views. Rank-zero continuations require a spatial stopping proof even in finite construction. Smaller stacks reserve an extra level for rounding; this does not change visible detail. An unfinished ray retries at coarser detail; a final conservative hull pass avoids reporting unfinished branches as empty space. This fallback is an approximation and is reported by diagnostic queries. CPU root selection has its own explicit budget and reports failure rather than dropping roots.

One GPU pass writes colour plus packed view-space normal/depth. The second pass applies the existing screen-space Soft or Strong corner shading. Koch facet lighting fades from its enclosing patch normal over one to three detail footprints. Contact shading fades below eight projected leaf pixels and disappears below two. These fades change shading only; hit positions and surface normals remain exact. Classic and Depth lighting, outlines, independently eased atmosphere, PNG export and shared camera links remain available.

Picking and collision clearance use small GPU query targets. Clearance traverses descendant hulls in every direction and returns a conservative lower bound, including unresolved geometry; it does not estimate distance from a few sampled rays. Incomplete clearance returns zero. Movement consumes the full elapsed interval in bounded steps and rechecks clearance between steps. It can resume after a previously unresolved gap opens.

Romanesco reuses a certified clearance ball between GPU queries. A distance lower bound at one eye remains valid after subtracting the distance travelled. Fixed-point subtraction precedes conversion to floating point, and the result scales with camera zoom. Reuse stops after consuming 20% of the original ball. Geometry changes invalidate it; zero or failed queries are never reused. This avoids repeated synchronous readbacks during elapsed-time catch-up without skipping movement or weakening collision bounds.

`query.ts` is a small CPU ray reference for validation. Production rendering, picking and clearance do not use it. The independent finite polygon oracle comes from the existing construction generators. Romanesco uses a separate finite analytic union and tests cone hits, conservative bounds, Fibonacci neighbour chains, connectivity and tiny-cone precision.

Exact-output performance comparisons:

- Save `utils/shaders/spatial-flight.frag` from the revision being compared into a baseline file.
- `QK_WIDTH=1280 QK_HEIGHT=720 QK_SAMPLES=16 QK_WARMUP=8 node scripts/check-quadratic-koch-performance.mjs BASELINE.frag /private/tmp/quadratic-performance.js` generates a Playwright callback. Run with `run-code --filename` and require `passed: true`.
- The callback alternates baseline/current draws in one WebGL context. Hardware timer queries measure the trace and production screen pass; one-pixel readback also records synchronized wall time. Compilation, captures and correctness probes are outside the measurements. `gl.finish()` alone does not synchronize Chromium reliably.
- Seven fixtures cover opening, rotation, close-up, both lighting styles, outlines and zoom depths 80/240. Every color and packed normal/depth byte must match. Picking, clearance and fallback diagnostics are compared separately. `QK_FILTER`, `QK_WIDTH`, `QK_HEIGHT`, `QK_SAMPLES` and `QK_WARMUP` bound reruns; `--candidate FILE` tests a standalone shader experiment.
- An optional third positional capture directory saves one `captures.json` download. Run `node scripts/check-quadratic-koch-performance.mjs --extract-captures CAPTURE_DIRECTORY/captures.json` to recover the original before/after PNGs. Capture failures are reported separately from completed correctness and timing results.

- Set `SPATIAL_SPECS=all` to compare all ten recursive constructions, or use comma-separated names from the harness. This mode independently reproduces the comparison baseline's quadratic specialization and Romanesco stack selection. Run bounded groups when collecting captures. `QK_GROWTH`, `QK_MAX_RANK` and `QK_VISIT_LIMIT` exercise partial construction and exhausted traversal paths; visit overrides affect both implementations and are test-only.
- `scripts/check-voxel-performance.mjs` provides paired shader measurements for all four voxel patterns, with `VOXEL_RAYS=9` covering full sample accumulation. Its style-specialization flag is experimental; no voxel shader changes were retained by this optimization.
- Finite construction faces use stable radix ordering over their existing Float32 depths. Equal depths preserve draw order, signed zeros remain ties, and NaNs keep the original comparator fallback. `node scripts/benchmark-face-depth-order.mjs` measures sorting; `node scripts/check-face-render-performance.mjs CALLBACK.js [CAPTURES]` compares complete Canvas2D draws against an independently restored native sort, including exact image bytes.

- 3D polylines retain growable projection and counting-sort buffers per canvas through a WeakMap. Clipping flags and active bin counts reset each draw; Float32 projection and stable stroke order remain unchanged. `node scripts/check-polyline-performance.mjs BASELINE_POLYLINE.ts CALLBACK.js [CAPTURES]` compares complete Lorenz, Rössler and Hilbert draws, including every moving frame and separately instrumented typed-array allocations. `tests/polyline-buffer.test.mjs` covers clipping, point-count changes, independent canvases and both depth-bin modes. Normalization reads indexed source points without allocating a typed-array view for each point.
