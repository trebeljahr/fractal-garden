# Recursive voxel exploration

`camera.ts` keeps global positions in BigInt fixed point, with 160 spare bits beyond the current subdivision scale. Precision follows physical surface clearance immediately, so moving toward a boundary keeps revealing smaller cells. `navigationLevel` is the continuous travel scale; it adapts to the surrounding passages and intended direction of travel. Saved links retain that scale plus the damped atmosphere scale and its velocity. Legacy `viewLevel` links remain readable. Restored positions inside solid geometry return to the opening.

For every subdivision level, the CPU sends the nearest grid plane relative to the camera, its child-cell width, and the base-3 child digit on the positive side of that plane. Choosing the nearest plane preserves tiny distances near distant ancestor boundaries. No large absolute camera coordinate reaches the shader. Far ancestor rules with constant occupancy in the local viewing volume are omitted; crossing digit 2 to 0 does not change these center-count rules.

The grid texture uses an explicit `highp` sampler: float-variable precision does not determine texture-read precision ([GLSL ES 1.00, section 8](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/es/2.0/GLSL_ES_Specification_1.00.pdf)). Face crossings and cached upper bounds add the integer boundary index before multiplying by cell width, preserving the nearest plane instead of subtracting and adding a huge ancestor width. Parallel ray axes use explicit selection rather than arithmetic `mix`; an overflowing unused quotient must not introduce NaN into face-normal comparisons.

The shader follows rays through recursive child grids. Empty cells skip to their exit face. Occupied cells descend until their projected size is about half a CSS pixel; smaller holes are not evaluated. The detail footprint compensates for framebuffer scale, so Retina displays and the pixel budget do not change the chosen geometry. Edge antialiasing retains the actual framebuffer pixel footprint. The near footprint follows local surface clearance, with a numerical floor in precision units. Render grids extend twelve subdivisions beyond the greater of precision and navigation scale, up to the global limit. Expensive grazing rays get one coarser retry. Retained ancestor cells are cached along each ray until their bounds or pixel cutoff change. Projected root bounds scissor traversal to potentially occupied pixels. No complete voxel mesh is allocated.

`atmosphere.ts` uses a separate visual scale. `smoothing.ts` critically damps it toward the travel scale during movement, so a collision-driven slowdown cannot instantly replace the whole fog volume. A bounded spring lead rejects abrupt direction changes; Shift increases that lead enough to follow sustained boosted refinement. Integer precision changes and GPU load do not alter physical fog density. Haze starts at 0.2 local travel units, with density 4 over a 96-unit horizon. One normalized exponential curve reaches the background at the horizon, avoiding a separate steep terminal fade. Releasing movement leaves both camera and atmosphere stationary.

Movement first traces a bounded sequence of empty passages along the requested direction. Each passage's physical width defines its target speed. Logistic recovery gradually increases speed when entering larger surroundings, easing acceleration to zero near the new cruising speed. Its maximum outward rate is 0.75 ternary levels per second. Directional lookahead starts braking before narrower passages and approaches retained walls exponentially, automatically refining the camera scale. Walls parallel to travel do not impose a clearance-based speed penalty. The integration splits at passage boundaries and speed transitions, using elapsed time independently of throttle and Shift boost. The actual travel scale stays separate from the fog state, so retreat after braking cannot jump to the wider atmosphere scale. Scroll/pinch also moves the camera and adjusts its current scale; it does not impose a permanent speed limit after leaving a small region. Collision and rendering resolve finer apertures as precision grows, without requiring wheel zoom. Collision uses double-precision relative planes and a small local face margin; unresolved segments stop conservatively.

Clearance queries do not allocate the render texture or collect grid levels. Collision sweeps collect grid levels without a render texture. Once `sqrt(3) * childWidth` is below the best bound, finer periodic rules cannot improve it and need no evaluation. Retained children are precomputed per pattern. The full renderer grid and movement bound agree; returning just the root-box distance near a tunnel opening would incorrectly prevent entering the shape.

Desktop flight uses Pointer Lock after a canvas click or Enter while the canvas is focused. Relative mouse movement turns the camera without dragging or screen-edge limits. Escape releases the mouse; Back outside ends Explore. Blur, context loss and unmount release capture and held keys. Browsers denying capture retain drag navigation, and touch gestures do not request a lock. Keyboard and touch flight use 0.75 local travel units per second, with a fourfold Shift boost; diagonal movement is normalized and collision traversal remains bounded. Held movement uses the full elapsed frame time. Frames longer than 0.5 seconds split into bounded collision sweeps with the same throttle, so expensive views cannot discard travel time. Blur and visibility changes clear held input before resuming. Space/C move along world up/down, even when looking vertically. Q/E remain aliases. Flight keys use physical key codes and consume events before page-navigation shortcuts. Wheel and pinch dolly stop at a fixed radius outside the whole shape; a new outward wheel gesture then scrolls the page through the shared momentum guard. Free keyboard flight can still move beyond that radius.

Explore offers independent **Lighting** (Classic / Depth) and **Corner shading** (Off / Soft / Minecraft AO) controls. The default is Depth with Soft shading. Shared links retain both choices; changing either leaves the camera in place. These controls affect the recursive flight renderer, not the separate Canvas2D construction view.

Both lighting modes use the actual intersected face normal once the face is fully resolved. Depth adds a warm key above and to the viewer’s left, cool ambient fill, near-field relief shading, the selected material colour, and cool middle-distance haze. Relief darkens receding layers smoothly toward a 55% floor before the existing haze. It uses axial hit depth in the damped visual travel scale, so a flat wall does not darken toward its screen edges. Outside the fractal, the fixed root’s front support plane starts this ramp; inside it, the eye does. Selecting that support corner directly preserves nearby ancestor planes at deep zoom. Solid outlines use the same illumination at 18% strength. The haze still reaches the selected background at the horizon. Material colour does not vary with subdivision depth. Newly resolved cavity faces blend from the last fully resolved face normal over one ternary subdivision (one to three detail footprints), reducing lighting changes at the cutoff. Exact hit normals still drive outlines and occupancy queries. Hidden faces and wire-only views retain Classic composition.

Both Mosely variants use two face materials in Explore: faces perpendicular to the X axis use 78% of the selected fill colour; the other faces keep the selected fill. The default blue pair is approximately `#8fb0ff` and `#7089c7`, before lighting and fog. The tint follows the face-lighting detail fade, stays attached to the geometry while turning, and applies in both lighting modes. Construction, Menger, Vicsek and wire-only views retain their existing colours.

Soft shading tests four neighbouring cells in the outside layer of the hit face and fades contact darkness over 18% of the face width. Clear face centres skip the neighbour scan. Minecraft AO checks eight neighbours, applies the two-side/one-diagonal corner rule, and bilinearly interpolates the four corner values across the face. Both use the finest retained voxel evaluated by traversal, so holes in enclosing parent cubes also remain open to ambient light. Using the last crossed cavity's larger cubes would shade exposed protrusions as if their neighbours were solid. Queries share an ancestor scan through the visible voxel's level; checking only that level would invent occluders across parent boundaries. Only ambient fill is reduced, leaving directly lit surfaces readable. No secondary rays are traced.

Shading positions use the exact crossed plane, without the traversal's forward epsilon. AO fades below eight framebuffer pixels of cell width and vanishes below two, and also fades with newly resolved face lighting; oversized cells fade out before a quarter of the render horizon so neighbour queries stay within the CPU's valid ancestor-pruning domain. Outer root faces have no local enclosing neighbours. This differs from the earlier binary probes whose radius followed pixel size and whose darkness affected all light.

Current limits: 256 global subdivision levels, camera levels 0–244, camera-relative coordinates capped at 1e29 local units for GPU arithmetic, at most 512 steps per ray with one coarser retry, and up to 2,500,000 framebuffer pixels (up to 2× device scale). `frameBudget.ts` samples consecutive rendered frame intervals, including CPU work and GPU presentation. Sustained intervals above 22.5 ms reduce the actual pixel workload; stalls above 50 ms react immediately. The floor is 160,000 pixels. A second of healthy cadence restores 10% of the budget, limiting resolution pumping. Idle callbacks cannot earn recovery, and render quality never changes the camera or its travel scale. Export draws use the full pixel budget. These are practical limits, not literal infinite geometry or a guaranteed frame rate. The whole-shape construction renderer remains available independently.

Mobile uses two independently captured touch sticks, following the Collection of Beauty move/look layout. A small deadzone and proportional deflection permit fine movement; the right stick turns continuously. Up/Down and held Fast buttons control height and speed. Multi-touch, pointer cancellation, lost capture, blur, visibility changes and unmount clear held input. Controls belong to the stage subtree, so they remain in fullscreen and scroll with the view.

The Mosely entry cameras fit all retained first-stage cubes from outside, centered with a margin in the shorter viewport dimension. Its Places to explore menu includes a face-on view and the airy lattice’s interior Koch opening. Menger and Vicsek retain their exterior entry views. Supported rules: Menger, seven-copy Vicsek, and both Mosely variants. Jerusalem and polyhedron flakes have different subdivision rules and do not use this path.

Effect cleanup deletes renderer resources immediately. Context release waits until the canvas leaves the DOM, so development Strict Mode and Fast Refresh can replay effects on the same canvas without losing its context. Real context loss pauses rendering; restoration recreates resources and draws the current camera. The stage keeps the selected background while graphics are unavailable.
