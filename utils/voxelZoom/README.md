# Recursive voxel exploration

`camera.ts` keeps the global camera in BigInt fixed point. A level defines the local unit as `2 / 3^level` world units. Precision grows with the level, retaining 160 extra bits. Camera links validate position, precision, level and orientation before allocating or parsing coordinates.

For every subdivision level, the CPU sends the nearest grid plane relative to the camera, its child-cell width, and the base-3 child digit on the positive side of that plane. Choosing the nearest plane preserves tiny distances near distant ancestor boundaries. No large absolute camera coordinate reaches the shader. Far ancestor rules with constant occupancy in the local viewing volume are omitted; crossing digit 2 to 0 does not change these center-count rules.

The shader follows rays through recursive child grids. Empty cells skip directly to their exit face. Occupied cells descend until their projected size is about one framebuffer pixel; holes smaller than that footprint are not evaluated. `resolution.ts` sets the pixel cone from the framebuffer and field of view. Its width grows with camera distance, with a near floor proportional to local clearance. Clearance, grid widths and ray distances share the camera's zoom-scaled units, so changing recursive levels preserves projected detail. Expensive grazing rays get one coarser retry. Distant geometry uses coarser voxels. `atmosphere.ts` adds a fixed world-space fog ramp from distance 1 to a far plane at distance 16 (the root cube spans 2 world units). The ramp reaches the selected background before clipping, hiding the far plane. These artistic distances never depend on clearance, frame time, framebuffer resolution or traversal pressure. Camera units convert the same physical fog and clipping distances at every recursive zoom level; deep numerical limits do not compress the fog ramp. The traversal allocates no complete voxel mesh. Ancestors that are constant throughout the numerical viewing range are omitted.

The movement bound is the maximum of distance to the root box and distance to each level's periodic union of retained children. Each is a lower bound on distance to the fractal. Neighboring child cells are included, so a continuous empty tunnel does not acquire artificial walls at subdivision boundaries. Movement takes bounded fractions of this safe distance. The local scale follows clearance, slowing world-space movement near surfaces.

Movement evaluates clearance without allocating the render texture or collecting grid levels. Once `sqrt(3) * childWidth` is below the best bound, finer periodic rules cannot improve it and need no evaluation. Retained children are precomputed per pattern. The full renderer grid and movement bound agree; returning just the root-box distance near a tunnel opening would incorrectly prevent entering the shape.

Desktop flight uses Pointer Lock after a canvas click or Enter while the canvas is focused. Relative mouse movement turns the camera without dragging or screen-edge limits. Escape releases the mouse; Back outside ends Explore. Blur, context loss and unmount release capture and held keys. Browsers denying capture retain drag navigation, and touch gestures do not request a lock. Keyboard flight runs at four clearance units per second, with a fourfold Shift boost; diagonal movement is normalized and collision substeps remain bounded. Space/C move along world up/down, even when looking vertically. Q/E remain aliases. Flight keys use physical key codes and consume events before page-navigation shortcuts. Wheel and pinch dolly stop at the initial camera radius; a new outward wheel gesture then scrolls the page through the shared momentum guard. Free keyboard flight can still move beyond that radius.

Lighting uses the actual intersected face normal with a directional key and ambient fill, matching the first flight renderer. Parent-cell lighting and binary ambient probes introduced large flat patches and abrupt lighting boundaries, so neither is used. Restrained outlines distinguish faces and recesses. Wire-only views retain full-strength fine outlines. This is local lighting, without global shadow rays.

Current limits: 256 global subdivision levels, camera levels 0–244, camera-relative coordinates capped at 1e29 local units for GPU arithmetic, at most 512 steps per ray with one coarser retry, and 2,500,000 framebuffer pixels (up to 2× device scale). These are practical limits, not literal infinite geometry or a guaranteed frame rate. The whole-shape construction renderer remains available independently.

Mobile uses two independently captured touch sticks, following the Collection of Beauty move/look layout. A small deadzone and proportional deflection permit fine movement; the right stick turns continuously. Up/Down and held Fast buttons control height and speed. Multi-touch, pointer cancellation, lost capture, blur, visibility changes and unmount clear held input. Controls belong to the stage subtree, so they remain in fullscreen and scroll with the view.

Supported rules: Menger, seven-copy Vicsek, and both Mosely variants. Jerusalem and polyhedron flakes have different subdivision rules and do not use this path.

Effect cleanup deletes renderer resources immediately. Context release waits until the canvas leaves the DOM, so development Strict Mode and Fast Refresh can replay effects on the same canvas without losing its context. Real context loss pauses rendering; restoration recreates resources and draws the current camera. The stage keeps the selected background while graphics are unavailable.

Validation: `node --test tests/voxel-flight.test.mjs tests/voxel-flight-resolution.test.mjs tests/voxel-flight-atmosphere.test.mjs tests/explorer-share.test.mjs`. `node scripts/check-voxel-flight-gpu.mjs /tmp/check-voxel-flight.js` generates a Playwright CLI payload comparing GPU rays to independent finite-box intersections and level-150 boundary/nested-copy fixtures. A second pass compares face brightness with independent finite-box entry normals; this catches parent-cell lighting regressions. The generated result must report `passed: true`. Keep the oracle recurrence/traversal independent; do not weaken its checks to accommodate a renderer mismatch.

Execute the generated payload with Playwright CLI `run-code --filename`. It requires development Strict Mode replay, checks all supported patterns, native mouse capture and look without dragging, Escape release, unmount release, resource cleanup and re-entry, context restoration and Mosely variant swaps. A production-only check cannot catch this replay regression.
