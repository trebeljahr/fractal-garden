# Recursive voxel exploration

`camera.ts` keeps the global camera in BigInt fixed point. A level defines the local unit as `2 / 3^level` world units. Precision grows with the level, retaining 160 extra bits. The independent `viewLevel` stores the user-selected exploration scale; precision levels may change near geometry without changing it. Camera links validate position, precision, both scales and orientation before allocating or parsing coordinates. Legacy links use their saved precision level as the exploration scale.

For every subdivision level, the CPU sends the nearest grid plane relative to the camera, its child-cell width, and the base-3 child digit on the positive side of that plane. Choosing the nearest plane preserves tiny distances near distant ancestor boundaries. No large absolute camera coordinate reaches the shader. Far ancestor rules with constant occupancy in the local viewing volume are omitted; crossing digit 2 to 0 does not change these center-count rules.

The shader follows rays through recursive child grids. Empty cells skip directly to their exit face. Occupied cells descend until their projected size is about one framebuffer pixel; holes smaller than that footprint are not evaluated. `resolution.ts` sets the pixel cone from the framebuffer and field of view. Its width grows with camera distance, with a near floor proportional to local clearance. Clearance, grid widths and ray distances share the camera's zoom-scaled units, so changing recursive levels preserves projected detail. Expensive grazing rays get one coarser retry. Distant geometry uses coarser voxels. `atmosphere.ts` adds exponential haze starting at 0.1 viewed-scale units, with density 7 over a 32-unit horizon. Haze is already visible at medium distances; the last quarter of the range fades fully into the selected background before clipping. The atmosphere uses the explicit exploration scale, which only wheel/pinch zoom changes. Ordinary flight, looking around, collision precision changes and idle frames cannot alter physical fog density or its horizon. No delayed density transition or idle settling redraw remains. Density and horizon never depend on frame time pressure, framebuffer resolution or traversal exhaustion. Detail resolution remains the compute control. The traversal allocates no complete voxel mesh. Ancestors that are constant throughout the numerical viewing range are omitted.

The movement bound is the maximum of distance to the root box and distance to each level's periodic union of retained children. Each is a lower bound on distance to the fractal. Neighboring child cells are included, so a continuous empty tunnel does not acquire artificial walls at subdivision boundaries. Wheel/pinch dolly uses bounded fractions of this safe distance to enter finer detail. It updates the continuous exploration scale from the physical clearance ratio, including camera-unit conversions. Keyboard and touch flight keep a constant displacement at the selected exploration scale. They sweep the requested segment through hierarchical empty cells, clipping only actual collisions. CPU collision planes use double precision and allocate no render texture; travelling parallel to a nearby wall does not slow movement. Conservative traversal exhaustion stops unresolved segments.

Clearance queries do not allocate the render texture or collect grid levels. Collision sweeps collect grid levels without a render texture. Once `sqrt(3) * childWidth` is below the best bound, finer periodic rules cannot improve it and need no evaluation. Retained children are precomputed per pattern. The full renderer grid and movement bound agree; returning just the root-box distance near a tunnel opening would incorrectly prevent entering the shape.

Desktop flight uses Pointer Lock after a canvas click or Enter while the canvas is focused. Relative mouse movement turns the camera without dragging or screen-edge limits. Escape releases the mouse; Back outside ends Explore. Blur, context loss and unmount release capture and held keys. Browsers denying capture retain drag navigation, and touch gestures do not request a lock. Keyboard and touch flight run at 0.75 exploration units per second, with a fourfold Shift boost; diagonal movement is normalized and collision traversal remains bounded. Elapsed movement time supports frames up to 0.5 seconds, so ordinary render slowdowns do not silently discard most travel time. Space/C move along world up/down, even when looking vertically. Q/E remain aliases. Flight keys use physical key codes and consume events before page-navigation shortcuts. Wheel and pinch dolly stop at the initial camera radius; a new outward wheel gesture then scrolls the page through the shared momentum guard. Free keyboard flight can still move beyond that radius.

Lighting uses the actual intersected face normal with a directional key and ambient fill, matching the first flight renderer. Parent-cell lighting and binary ambient probes introduced large flat patches and abrupt lighting boundaries, so neither is used. Restrained outlines distinguish faces and recesses. Wire-only views retain full-strength fine outlines. This is local lighting, without global shadow rays.

Current limits: 256 global subdivision levels, camera levels 0–244, camera-relative coordinates capped at 1e29 local units for GPU arithmetic, at most 512 steps per ray with one coarser retry, and 2,500,000 framebuffer pixels (up to 2× device scale). These are practical limits, not literal infinite geometry or a guaranteed frame rate. The whole-shape construction renderer remains available independently.

Mobile uses two independently captured touch sticks, following the Collection of Beauty move/look layout. A small deadzone and proportional deflection permit fine movement; the right stick turns continuously. Up/Down and held Fast buttons control height and speed. Multi-touch, pointer cancellation, lost capture, blur, visibility changes and unmount clear held input. Controls belong to the stage subtree, so they remain in fullscreen and scroll with the view.

Supported rules: Menger, seven-copy Vicsek, and both Mosely variants. Jerusalem and polyhedron flakes have different subdivision rules and do not use this path.

Effect cleanup deletes renderer resources immediately. Context release waits until the canvas leaves the DOM, so development Strict Mode and Fast Refresh can replay effects on the same canvas without losing its context. Real context loss pauses rendering; restoration recreates resources and draws the current camera. The stage keeps the selected background while graphics are unavailable.

A second pass compares face brightness with independent finite-box entry normals; this catches parent-cell lighting regressions. The generated result must report `passed: true`. Keep the oracle recurrence/traversal independent; do not weaken its checks to accommodate a renderer mismatch.

It compares measured displacement with elapsed frame time; it does not infer speed from shader uniforms. Execute with `run-code --filename`; the result must report `passed: true`.
